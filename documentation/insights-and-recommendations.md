# Insights and Recommendations

## Business question

**What are the biggest drivers of customer churn, and which customer groups should Horizon prioritise for retention?**

## Overall churn position

- Total customers: **7,043**
- Churned customers: **1,869**
- Overall churn rate: **26.5%**
- Monthly revenue at risk: **£139,131**
- Average monthly charge for churned customers: approximately **£74**
- Average monthly charge for retained customers: approximately **£61**

The analysis indicates that churn is concentrated in particular customer groups rather than evenly distributed across the customer base.

## Finding 1 - Contract and tenure

Month-to-month customers had a churn rate of **42.7%**. Customers in their first 12 months had a churn rate of **47.4%**, with churn reducing progressively across longer tenure bands.

### Recommendation

Create an early-life retention journey and test incentives that encourage suitable month-to-month customers to move onto longer contracts.

## Finding 2 - Payment method

Electronic check customers had the highest payment-related churn rate at **45.3%**. Automatic bank transfer and automatic credit-card customers had much lower churn rates of approximately **15-17%**.

### Recommendation

Encourage electronic check customers to adopt automatic payment through simpler setup, targeted prompts and appropriate incentives.

### Data-quality note

The source lookup table did not define payment code 5. **21 records** were therefore reported as `Unknown` rather than being assigned to an unsupported payment category.

## Finding 3 - Support and service add-ons

Customers without support and security services showed higher churn:

- No Tech Support: approximately **41.6%**
- No Online Security: approximately **41.8%**
- No Online Backup: approximately **39.9%**
- Fibre optic customers: **41.9%**

### Recommendation

Test fibre optic propositions that include Tech Support, Online Security and Online Backup.

## Finding 4 - High-risk customer groups

The highest-priority combined segment identified was:

**Month-to-month + Fibre optic: 54.6% churn**

Other individual signals included:

- Senior citizens: **41.7%**
- No partner: **33.0%**
- No dependents: **31.3%**
- Paperless billing: **33.6%**
- Gender: little difference relative to the overall churn rate

### Recommendation

Prioritise customers with **multiple risk factors** rather than relying on one broad retention campaign.

## Proposed retention priorities

1. Month-to-month customers in their first 12 months
2. Month-to-month fibre optic customers
3. Electronic check customers
4. Fibre optic customers without support/security add-ons
5. Customers showing several churn-risk signals at the same time

## Measuring the next step

In a live retention programme I would track:

- churn rate by target segment before and after intervention
- contract-conversion rate
- automatic-payment migration rate
- add-on adoption rate
- retained monthly revenue
- campaign response and cost per retained customer

This would allow the recommendations to be tested rather than treated as assumptions.
